[🇫🇷 Français](README.md) | 🇬🇧 English

**[🧪 Live dashboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/marinedde/molprint-dashboard)** — screen a molecule, browse the shortlisted candidates, explore the analyzed genes, and check the methodology/rigor page.

**[📖 Full guide](docs/GUIDE_COMPLET.md)** — the whole project explained step by step (French only for now) — analogies, code, results, SQL/Python/ML for beginners, plus a full technical/architecture audit and a data leakage/overfitting check.

In silico drug-response prediction project, built as a companion to [OncoPrint](https://github.com/marinedde/cdsd-certification/tree/main/bloc6-direction-projet/oncoprint) (breast cancer molecular subtype classification, TCGA-BRCA, XGBoost + SHAP).

Goal: cover the bioinformatics / chemoinformatics / systems biology skills required for Data Scientist — Preclinical roles in pharma R&D (target market: Switzerland, Basel).

## Phase 2 — Chemoinformatics

Therapeutic target: **HER2/ERBB2** (chosen because it connects directly to OncoPrint's "HER2-enriched" subtype — trastuzumab is a reference targeted therapy for it).

1. `notebooks/01_bioactivity_data_acquisition.ipynb` — retrieves molecules tested against ERBB2 via the PubChem BioAssay API ("bioactivity concise" table). ChEMBL was the original target, but its API was down (HTTP 500 errors) at the time of writing.
2. `notebooks/02_rdkit_descriptors_qsar.ipynb` — molecular descriptors (RDKit, Lipinski's rules) and a QSAR model (XGBoost + SHAP) predicting a molecule's activity from its structure.
3. `notebooks/03_virtual_screening_optimization.ipynb` — virtual screening and in silico optimization: the model is validated against 5 approved anti-HER2 drugs (predicted probability consistent with their known-inhibitor status), then new candidates are generated by fragment recombination (BRICS) from the most active molecules, filtered for structural diversity (Butina clustering on Morgan fingerprints), and a top 15 shortlist is selected.
4. `notebooks/04_fingerprint_qsar.ipynb` — cross-validated comparison of three molecular representations (descriptors alone, Morgan fingerprints alone, and both combined); the combined model (ROC AUC 0.98) becomes the final model, noticeably more reliable on the reference drugs than the descriptors-only model.
5. `notebooks/06_scaffold_validation_applicability_domain.ipynb` — **methodological rigor**: the random split (ROC AUC 0.98) is optimistic because the dataset contains many close analogs (499 unique chemical scaffolds for 1070 molecules). A Bemis-Murcko scaffold split (the field's standard practice, as in the MoleculeNet benchmark) gives an honest ROC AUC of 0.91 on scaffolds never seen in training (active-class recall 0.59) — and an applicability-domain check (Tanimoto similarity to the training set) shows the candidates generated in notebook 03 remain in a "moderately novel" zone, so their scores are leads to investigate, not reliable predictions on their own.
6. `notebooks/09_exploratory_data_analysis.ipynb` — **EDA done in hindsight**: healthy class balance (51%/49%), 0 invalid SMILES, one genuine structural outlier (labeled inactive), strong multicollinearity among size-related descriptors — and, most importantly, active molecules are on average 44% heavier than inactive ones. Combined with a label-permutation test (shuffled labels → AUC drops to ~0.5, ruling out leakage) and the analysis of the single most important fingerprint bit (a generic kinase-inhibitor motif — see the guide's data-leakage section), this shows the model mostly discriminates "looks like a kinase inhibitor" rather than fine-grained HER2 selectivity.

## Phase 4 — Internal database

Links, in a single SQLite database (`data/processed/molprint.db`), the pieces built separately across phases: genes (Phase 1), candidate molecules (Phase 2), the signaling pathway (Phase 3), and an independent external source — **GDSC** (Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer, Sanger Institute), which measures the real, lab-verified sensitivity of 51 breast-cancer cell lines to hundreds of drugs.

`notebooks/08_internal_database.ipynb`:
- 7 tables: `genes`, `subtypes`, `pathways`, `pathway_genes`, `molecules`, `cell_lines`, `drug_response`
- The 51 GDSC cell lines are annotated by molecular subtype (a manual classification from the literature — Neve et al. 2006, Kao et al. 2009 — deliberately incomplete: undocumented lines are left "unclassified" rather than guessed)
- The original roadmap's target query: *"for a given subtype, which key genes, which candidate molecules, which signaling pathway?"*
- **Independent empirical validation**: the GDSC data (real, unrelated to our own models) confirms that lapatinib is ~10x more potent on HER2-enriched cell lines (mean IC50 ≈ 1.8 µM) than on Luminal A or Triple Negative ones (≈ 17-21 µM) — consistent with the Phase 2 and Phase 3 hypotheses.

## Phase 3 — Systems biology

**HER2 → PI3K → AKT** signaling pathway (the core of proliferation signaling in the "HER2-enriched" subtype), modeled with ordinary differential equations (ODEs) via Tellurium — a 3-tier kinase cascade, each protein switching between an inactive and an active state.

`notebooks/07_systems_biology_her2_pathway.ipynb`:
- Simulates the cascade untreated, then with HER2 blocked (lapatinib-like) — the signal shuts down across all three tiers
- **Resistance mechanism**: an activating *PIK3CA* mutation (a clinically documented trastuzumab-resistance mechanism) keeps PI3K active independently of HER2 — even with HER2 ~95% blocked, AKT stays almost as active as with no treatment at all
- A generic simulator to pick which node to block (HER2, PI3K, or AKT) and compare against the untreated signal
- A dose-response curve: intensity of HER2 blockade vs. steady-state AKT activity

Kinetic constants are illustrative (not fitted on experimental data) — the goal is to demonstrate the modeling method, not to produce a validated quantitative biological result.

## Phase 1 — RNA/DNA sequences

Genes chosen: the 5 top SHAP features in OncoPrint, one per breast cancer subtype (ESR1, ERBB2, FOXA1, AR, GATA3) — directly anchored on already-produced results rather than an arbitrary gene selection.

`notebooks/05_gene_sequence_analysis.ipynb` (functions in `src/sequence_analysis.py`):
- Retrieves canonical RefSeq sequences (NCBI, `Bio.Entrez`), GC content, DNA → protein translation, physicochemical protein descriptors (molecular weight, instability index, isoelectric point)
- Verifies, directly in the translated sequence, that position 537 of ESR1 does carry a tyrosine — the exact site of the **Y537S** endocrine-resistance mutation, one of the best documented in metastatic breast cancer — then simulates the mutation and compares physicochemical properties before/after.
